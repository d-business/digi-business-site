## Where the numbers come from

The numbers behind your report come from Google itself.

We use search volume data through a platform called DataForSEO. It's the same kind of data advertisers see when they plan Google Ads: how many people search a term, month by month, in a given area.

That matters for one reason. It's Google-wide.

Some demand reports in this market are built from a single marketplace's own traffic. Useful, but it only shows what happens on that one site. It can't tell you what everyone in your county is typing into Google.

Ours can. That's why your report breaks down to your area, not just the national total.

Here's the honest part, and it's the part most providers skip. These volumes are estimates, grouped into rounded bands. A term might show as 1,900 searches a month when the true figure sits a little above or below.

They're directional, not precise to the last search. For spotting what buyers want and when, that's exactly enough. For claiming a figure to the decimal point, it isn't, and we won't pretend otherwise.

We pull fresh numbers each time, and note the date we pulled them. Search demand moves with the seasons, so a report built on last year's figures would quietly mislead you.

## How we draw your area

"Your area" needs a definition, so here's ours.

We take roughly 50 miles around your dealership and build it from whole counties. We start with your own county, then add the neighbours whose main towns sit inside that radius. For most dealers that's three to six counties.

Why counties, and not a neat circle on a map? Because the search data comes in county-sized blocks. There's no dial for "47 miles from my forecourt". Counties are the closest honest match.

It's an approximation, and we say so. A circle drawn with a compass would clip the edge of a county here and there. The county method counts the whole thing.

So we list every county we used, right there in your report. You see exactly what "your area" means, with nothing buried in the method.

If you sit on a border, or cover more ground than most, we widen the ring and tell you we did.

## How we check where you rank

Knowing what people search for is half the picture. The other half is whether they find you.

So we take the 8 to 10 searches with the most buying intent, the "for sale" and "near me" terms, and run them live on Google. On a mobile, because that's where buyers research: on the sofa, at night, phone in hand.

For each one, we record three things.

Where you rank, from page one to not in the top twenty. Where each competitor ranks on the same search. And whether a local map pack shows up, with the pins Google drops at the top of the results.

Then a quick strength check. We compare how many searches you rank for against your main rivals, so you can see the gap in plain numbers.

One note on the big marketplaces. Auto Trader and the like will often sit above everyone. We set them aside when we name your local rivals, but we flag them once, because they take clicks you could be winning yourself.

## What the data can't tell you

Every report has limits. Most hide them. We'd rather you knew ours up front.

The search volumes are estimates in rounded bands, not exact counts. We've said it once, and it's worth saying twice.

Low-volume searches show as "too low to measure". That's not the same as zero. In a quiet rural county, a niche term may get searched and still slip under the radar.

It's a snapshot, not a full audit. Three or four pages show you the shape of your market and the obvious gaps. The deeper work, page by page and keyword by keyword, is what a proper project is for.

It shows demand and visibility, not sales. The report can tell you a buyer searched and found you. It can't tell you what they did next, or whether your website turned them into an enquiry.

And it measures Google, not AI. More buyers will ask ChatGPT and the like over time. For now, most still start on Google, so that's where we look. Worth preparing for the shift. No need to panic about it yet.

County areas approximate a radius. They don't replace one.

None of that weakens the report. It just tells you what it is: a clear, honest read of your market, with its edges marked.
