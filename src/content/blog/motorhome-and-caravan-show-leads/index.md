Nearly 96,000 people came through the Motorhome and Caravan Show at the NEC last October. Most of them left without placing an order.

That is not a failure. It is how big-ticket buying works. A caravan or motorhome gets slept on, talked over at home, measured against the drive.

The orders you take on the stand are real and worth having. But the bigger prize is the crowd who leave to research and decide later.

Whoever they find online gets the sale. It should be you.

## The short version

Most people who visit the Motorhome and Caravan Show go home and research online before they buy. Dealers win those buyers by having their model pages, used stock pages, a show-offer page and a fast enquiry path ready before the doors open.

Three steps to do it:

1. Get your pages ready before 13 October: model pages, used stock, a show-offer page, finance.
2. Capture details at the show, then send a useful follow-up within days.
3. Track which enquiries came from the show, and repeat the whole thing for February.

## Plenty order on the stand. Many more go home to think

Let me be clear first. Dealers take real orders at the show, and good ones.

At last year's event Coachman reported caravan sales up 12% and motorhome sales up 83%. People do buy.

But for every order written on a stand, a far larger group walks away without signing. That is rarely cold feet. It is the size of the decision.

A van at 30 to 70 thousand pounds gets slept on. It gets talked over at the kitchen table, then weighed against the space on the drive.

Those undecided visitors are the opportunity. They are interested, they have seen your stock, and they are about to spend weeks researching online.

One exhibitor put it well after last year's show. PJ Outdoors noted a high level of people researching their products before buying, and expected that research to turn into sales by the February show.

So the show starts the journey. Your website finishes it, for everyone who does not order on the day.

## The six weeks after the show decide your return

The order book on the Sunday night is not the whole story. The real test is the six weeks that follow.

From the close on 18 October to the run-up to Christmas, your buyers are deciding. They look up the exact model they sat in, read the reviews and compare layouts.

They search for a dealer they can trust, often with "near me" on the end. They check what the finance might cost.

Then demand dips over Christmas, as you would expect. In January it comes back hard, running straight into the February show.

Here is the catch. If you are not visible while all that research is happening, the buyer builds a shortlist without you on it.

By the time they are ready to walk back onto a forecourt, the decision is half made. You want to be the dealer they already trust, not the one they never found.

## What your website needs ready before 13 October

This is the practical part. None of it is clever. It is just done properly, before the doors open.

Think of it the way you build a good stand. Everything a visitor needs is where they expect it, nothing is in the way, and it is obvious what to do next.

Your website is the stand they visit at nine o'clock that night, from the sofa. This is what has to be ready.

### A "see us at the NEC" page

One page, one web address, for everyone who meets you at the show. Put it on your stand signage, your badges, a card, a QR code.

When a visitor photographs your board and looks you up that evening, they land somewhere built for them. Not your general homepage.

That page can carry your show offers, the models you have on the stand, and an easy way to enquire.

### Proper model pages for everything on your stand

If a visitor searches the exact model they liked, your page should be the best one they find. Original words, real photos, honest detail.

Copying the manufacturer's text is the common mistake. When your page is thin or duplicated, you lose to the brand's own site and to other dealers.

Write for the buyer comparing two or three layouts at the kitchen table. Answer the questions they actually ask.

### Used stock pages that survive the show

Shows shift a lot of stock, new and used. When a unit sells, do not let its page drop into a dead link.

A sold page that returns an error throws away the ranking and traffic you built. Redirect it, or keep it working with a clear "now sold, here is something similar".

More used stock comes to market after a show. The dealers who treat used pages as proper pages, not throwaways, pick up those searches.

### A fast, clear way to enquire, including finance

Most of this research happens on a phone, at night. If enquiring takes more than a moment, you lose them.

One obvious button and a short form. A phone number you can tap with a thumb.

Finance matters here too. Plenty of buyers want the monthly cost before they commit, so a clear finance page earns enquiries that are expensive to buy through ads.

One word of care. Finance is regulated, so keep the page to marketing, give no advice, and get the wording checked against FCA rules before it goes live.

## After the show: a follow-up that is not a big project

You do not need new software to do this well. You need speed and a little discipline.

Capture the details of everyone who shows real interest, at the stand or through your show page. A name and an email is enough to start.

Then follow up within days, while the visit is fresh. Send something useful, not a hard sell: the spec they asked about, a clear next step, an answer to their question.

A visitor who gets a prompt, helpful reply remembers it. Most dealers go quiet for weeks, and that gap is exactly what you can win.

Then watch which enquiries trace back to the show. That tells you what the week was really worth, and what to do more of next time.
