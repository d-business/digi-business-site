## Why copying the manufacturer's brochure quietly kills the page

Here's the mistake almost everyone makes. The 2027 range drops, the dealer wants pages up fast, so they paste the manufacturer's description and move on.

Twelve other dealers do exactly the same thing.

Now Google is looking at a dozen near-identical pages, all running the same words from the same brochure. It has no reason to pick yours. And if it has to pick one, it picks the brand's own site, not a small dealer copying it.

Google has said this plainly for years. Its guidance rewards original, people-first content and filters out copies that add nothing. Bing says the same in its webmaster guidelines. The AI answer tools work the same way: they lift and cite the page that says something of its own, not the fourteenth copy of a spec sheet.

So copied copy isn't neutral. It holds the page down.

The fix is the part only you can do. Real photos of the actual van, not the manufacturer's render that sits on fifty other sites. An honest line on who the layout suits and who it doesn't. Your price, your availability, part-exchange and finance in plain terms.

The manufacturer can't write the local, in-stock, been-in-it version. You can. That's your advantage, and most dealers leave it sitting unused.

## Your 2027 launch-page checklist

None of this needs a big project or new software. It needs a few things done well, this week, while the launch is still hot. Here's the list.

### One original page for every model you stock

One page per 2027 model, written from the spec sheet, not pasted from the brochure. Specs are the start. The value is the human layer: who the layout suits, what stands out, the honest trade-off. A couple after a fixed bed and a big washroom need to know, fast, that this is the one.

### Publish early, before demand peaks

Get the page up the week the model is announced, not when the first unit lands on the forecourt. Early pages get found and gather a little age before the searches climb. A good page live late loses to an average page live early. Publish it, link to it, make sure Google can crawl it.

### Use the words buyers actually type

Put the brand, the model and the year where they count: the title, the H1, the URL and the first line. "Swift Challenger 2027 [layout] for sale in [your town]" beats a clever name every time. Add the berth and your area. Match the search. Don't invent your own language for it.

### Show real photos and real detail

The manufacturer's render is on every other site already. Your photos of the actual van, and the detail only you hold, are both the originality signal and the reason someone enquires. Price, availability, ex-demo timing, what's included: the specifics do the selling.

### Make the enquiry path fast and obvious

Launch-season research happens on the sofa, on a phone. Give them one clear "check availability" or "enquire" action, easy to find, no hunting. Keep finance nearby and factual, with no advice, so the high-intent buyer has somewhere to go.

### Don't bin last year's pages

When the 2026 unit sells, don't let the page 404. Redirect it or repurpose it, so the authority it built keeps working. More used stock enters the market after every launch, and those pages matter more than most dealers think.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do 2027 caravan and motorhome models launch in the UK?

The new ranges are revealed around the October NEC show, which runs 13 to 18 October 2026, and roll out through the winter to the February show. Several manufacturers, including Coachman and Swift, publish their 2027 brochures ahead of it.

### Should dealers write their own model copy or use the manufacturer's?

Write your own. Copied manufacturer text rarely ranks, because Google and the AI answer tools filter out pages that only repeat what's already published elsewhere. Original copy, with your photos, price and availability, is what gets found and cited.

### What's the single most important thing to do at launch?

Publish an original, findable page for each model you stock, early. The first dealer with a proper page for a specific 2027 model wins the early searches, before demand peaks.
